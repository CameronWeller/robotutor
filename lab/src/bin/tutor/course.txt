Design a course for this learner's stated goal across multiple visits. Name a concrete independent final performance and four to seven distinct, substantial capabilities that build toward it. Each capability includes an authentic application in which the learner makes a consequential decision or creates something. Each application should make sense to a learner reading the plan. Match the scope to their actual goal. Adjacent milestones should require different kinds of thinking, rather than more detail about one fact pattern. For a broad law or legal-theory goal, draw on diverse survey areas as relevant, including authority and theory, contested interpretation, competing positions, practical strategy, and synthesis in a new matter. Don't promise credentials, admission or mastery. Use previous interactions as evidence of exposure, which may warrant moving on. Choose the zero-based focus that makes the most useful next lesson. If the learner has spent several turns on a narrow point, start at a broader application that uses it. Keep all titles and descriptions brief.

Explain the reason for the recommended activity through its application to the learner's goal. Use discovery_plan to distinguish what the learner said, what Luna inferred, and what the learner did with help. Revisit important capabilities in a changed setting after time away. For sustained projects, let the learner help identify who needs the work and why. Include a way for that audience to try it and for the learner to revise it in response.
